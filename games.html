Board Games 棋弈

Tony Tong 唐仪 has played WeiQi and Chinese chess all his life. In RuDao he uses the board to make a simple point: how you behave under pressure is who you are. Here are the two boards from that chapter - playable, with a friendly table companion.

WeiQi / Go 围棋

Surround territory, keep your groups alive, and never slam the stones. Pass twice to end and count. White receives 6.5 komi.

Black to play. Place a stone on any crossing.

    Chinese Chess 象棋

    Red moves first. Select a piece, then tap a marked point. The horse has a leg, the elephant an eye, the cannon needs a screen.

    Red to play. Select a piece to see its legal moves.

      Why a board game on a book site?

      In Chapter 4.3, Tony Tong tells the story of Liu Qi, who in a fit of temper struck a fellow prince dead with a LiuBo board - a lapse of manners at the board that grew into the Rebellion of the Seven States. The lesson is not about chess technique. It is about LiYi 礼仪: voluntary restraint, respect for rules, and self-command under pressure.

      So these boards are here to be played, not just admired. Play a quiet game, watch how the position changes when you rush, and then open the chat panel and ask the book what any of it has to do with a boardroom. That is the whole exercise: 从棋盘到董事会.
