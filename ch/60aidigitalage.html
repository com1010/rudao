Chapter 6.0

RuDao in the AI and Digital Age#

数字时代

From Information to Intelligence

智

I have lived through three revolutions: the Internet, blockchain, and now artificial intelligence. The Internet connected information. Blockchain connected value and trust. AI is connecting intelligence itself. Each one changed how we work, how we communicate, how we build organizations. None of them changed the question: how do we keep human wisdom in step with technological power? That question sits at the heart of RuDao.

Technology Changes, Human Nature Endures

AI can draft a report, write code, read a scan, translate a contract in seconds. It cannot answer the questions that decide whether any of it is used well: how trust is built, how power is exercised, how wealth is created responsibly. Technology increases capability; it does not determine values. A model can produce an answer in a millisecond. Only a person can decide which question is worth asking, and only a person can be held responsible when the answer is used.

Trust Becomes More Important, Not Less

Industrial age: physical assets. Information age: knowledge. Digital age: trust. When every competitor has the same models and the same data, the only thing that cannot be copied is the record of having kept your word. Trustworthiness is not a soft virtue. It is strategic capital.

Human with Machine

The future is not human versus machine. It is human with machine. AI processes faster; people supply judgment. AI finds patterns; people supply meaning. AI optimizes; people decide which values the optimization must serve.

From Competition to Co-opetition

No firm succeeds alone in a network economy. Co-opetition means compete where necessary, collaborate where possible, and create value together whenever you can. The aim is not to win a transaction but to build relationships that outlast it.

From Artificial Intelligence to Human Wisdom 从人工智能到人类智慧: 数字文明的儒道操作系统

When I reflect on the rapid convergence of Artificial Intelligence, Web3 trust architectures, and global capital markets, I arrive at a surprisingly simple conclusion: technology scales capability, but capability alone does not guarantee human progress. Throughout history, every major technological leap has expanded human power. The Industrial Revolution multiplied our physical capacity; the Information Revolution multiplied our access to data; Artificial Intelligence now multiplies our cognitive power. Yet none of these technologies can answer the fundamental questions that govern long-term survival: What is the right thing to do? What kind of society should we build? What responsibilities accompany unprecedented power? What values should guide intelligence?

These are not technical questions - they are human questions that have endured for millennia. This is precisely where the strategic framework of RuDao (儒道) becomes indispensable. For over two thousand years, Confucian and Daoist traditions have recognized that the ultimate purpose of knowledge is not mere information, but wisdom; the purpose of leadership is not raw power, but ethical responsibility; and the purpose of prosperity is not endless accumulation, but human flourishing. Artificial Intelligence changes nearly everything in our external environment, but human nature remains unchanged: trust still matters, personal character still matters, and moral virtue still matters. In fact, the more powerful our technologies become, the more critical these timeless principles are to our survival.

The true challenge of the Digital Civilization Age is therefore not merely the regulation of machines, but the governance of ourselves. The Industrial Age required machines, and the Information Age required technical knowledge; the Digital Civilization Age requires profound human wisdom. This is the core essence of what I define as The Fifth Modernization (第五现代化). While traditional modernization frameworks focused on agriculture, industry, national defense, and science and technology, the Fifth Modernization focuses on the foundational software of civilization: Trust (信), Virtue (德), Ethical Governance (义), Human-AI Collaboration (人机协同), and Digital Civilization (数字文明). The ultimate objective is not simply smarter machines, but a wiser global civilization.

Let me end where the board ends. In Go, the final phase is not conquest but counting - the board is full, and what remains is the shape you built. A business career counts the same way: not the quarters you won, but the shape you built - the partners who stayed, the promises that outlived the contracts, the children who learned the game's manners at your table. The machines will now play the openings faster than any of us. The endgame is still ours: move with the virtue of the inner sage, and the benefit of the outer king will follow.

Confucian Virtue Ethics and AI Business Governance

AI is not a value-neutral instrument. Behind every algorithm stands a human choice; behind every dataset stands a human bias. When AI enters the core of business - hiring, credit, medicine, marketing, risk control - ethics is no longer an option; it is the floor of survival.

Four Real Pain Points of Commercial AI

Algorithmic bias. Training data carries humanity's historical prejudice, producing unfair outcomes in hiring, credit, marketing, and risk control. Do not let AI discriminate against others.

Profit-only optimization. Most commercial AI optimizes solely for short-term revenue, conversion, and clicks - replicating the ordinary merchant's "profit first, righteousness after," amplified exponentially by the algorithm. "The gentleman understands righteousness; the petty person understands only profit." An algorithm that maximizes profit alone becomes the "petty person" of the digital age.

Missing humanity; floating accountability. When AI decisions go wrong, firms often deflect: "the algorithm's fault." Confucian Xin requires traceable responsibility and kept promises. "A person without trustworthiness - I do not know what they are good for." If AI can escape accountability, the enterprise credit system collapses.

The black-box moral vacuum. Large-model reasoning cannot be audited; in critical business decisions this creates a moral vacuum. "Without compass and square, one cannot draw circles and squares." AI without rules and audit is a compass-less drawing - disorder must follow.

The Doctrine of the Mean: Constraining Single-Objective Optimization

AI design should abandon "single-objective maximization" for "multi-objective dynamic balance":

The essence of AI ethics is the Doctrine of the Mean for the technological age: impartiality, dynamic balance.

Technical illustration:

Traditional model: maximize profit = Max(Revenue - Cost)

RuShang model: Max(Revenue - Cost - α × Bias_Score - β × Harm_Score)

where α and β are moral-constraint weights set by the board.

Core conclusion: the two are not opposites but complements. The Western framework supplies clear rules and processes; the Confucian framework supplies deep humanistic care and cultural self-awareness. The best AI governance is "Western rule frameworks + Eastern moral core."

Reader's Action Checklist:

  • Build a corporate AI-ethics self-audit covering bias, data privacy, and accountability.
  • Establish an AI ethics committee with veto power.
  • Draft the "AI must-not-do list": scenarios where fully autonomous AI decisions are prohibited.
  • Mandate human-in-the-loop review in all high-risk AI scenarios.
  • Run regular "empathy tests": examine algorithmic fairness from the most vulnerable stakeholder's perspective.
  • AI needs an ethical base; so does Web3. The blockchain can record everything - but it cannot guarantee the truth of what is entered.

Closing

Every model discussed in this chapter will be obsolete within five years. The questions will not. Someone will still have to decide what the machine is for, sign a name to what it does, and answer for it when it is wrong. Capability will keep arriving on schedule; accountability has to be built by hand.

The digital age does not need more intelligence. There is plenty of it, and there will be more. It needs people who can be trusted with it. RuDao is therefore not a philosophy of technology. It is a discipline for the person who deploys it: cultivate yourself first, then act in the world, and let the machine work for the civilization you are trying to leave behind.
