7.4

HuaXue-EnXue -- Modern RuShang Academy

华学恩学 -- 现代儒商教育体系

I first encountered HuaXue-EnXue (华学恩学) while searching for a practical way to connect classical Confucian wisdom with modern business, leadership, and daily life. What I found was not merely a course of study, but a living system of self-cultivation rooted in the traditions of Confucianism, Daoism, and Buddhism.

From 2024 to 2026, I had the privilege of attending several lectures by Master Xiao XianHua (肖先华), founder of HuaXue-EnXue. During the session, he shared a simple yet profound principle:

Live with poetic grace (诗情画意) and conduct oneself according to the spirit of ritual and harmony (礼乐).

What struck me most was that he was not speaking about philosophy as an abstract subject. He was speaking about how to live, how to lead, how to build families, organizations, and communities with greater character, balance, and meaning.

Master Xiao XianHua, a descendant of the distinguished Xiao family lineage associated with Xiao He (萧何), the renowned Han Dynasty statesman, has developed HuaXue-EnXue into a modern educational system that translates classical Chinese wisdom into practical guidance for contemporary life.

At its core, HuaXue-EnXue seeks to answer a question that also lies at the heart of RuDao:

How can ancient wisdom remain relevant in a rapidly changing modern world?

Its teaching framework -- "One Dao, Two Roots, Three Hearts, Four Refrainings, Five Questions, Six Harmonies, Seven Essentials, Eight Discernments, Nine Emptinesses, Ten Virtues, and Twenty Methods" -- provides a structured path for personal cultivation, family harmony, leadership development, and ethical entrepreneurship.

As I studied this framework, I was struck by how naturally it resonated with the principles of RuDao (儒道). Both emphasize character before success, virtue before power, and self-cultivation before leadership. Both seek to transform Confucian wisdom from a subject of study into a way of life.

In many respects, HuaXue-EnXue provides a practical cultivation system, while RuDao extends those principles into entrepreneurship, leadership, co-opetition, and Digital Civilization.

Together, they illustrate a timeless idea:

Education is not merely about acquiring knowledge.
It is about cultivating character, wisdom, and the ability to live with purpose.

Key mottos, principles, and concepts HuaXue by Professor Xiao XianHua within his lectures and volunteer training guidelines include:

1. The Core Pillar Core Values: three central attitudes:

"With a heart of gratitude, a heart of respect, and a heart for the public good. " "以感恩心,以恭敬心,以天下为公心"

Prof Xiao instills an active attitude of selflessness and humility:

"Only show gratitude, never complain; only show respect, never be arrogant, and let go of the ego. "

"只感恩,不抱怨;只恭敬,不傲慢,不我执。"

"Treat people well, only give gratitude, and do not hold grievances. "

"对人好,只感恩不抱怨。"

"Stand high upon the mountain peak, but walk deeply along the ocean floor - meaning to maintain noble aspirations while executing with absolute humility. "

"高高山顶立,低低海底行。"

"Only when your heart is dedicated to the public good can true success be achieved. "

"天下为公,才能成功。"

Dr. Tong Yan Kai ( Tang EnJia湯恩佳博士 香港孔教学院院长 世界儒商联合会会长), former industrialist and President of the Confucian Academy ( ConfucianAcademy.com ) in Hong Kong, is one of the most important modern advocates for RuShang ethics. His famous definition distinguishes the RuShang from ordinary merchants: "The greatest difference between a RuShang and an ordinary merchant is that the RuShang attaches great importance to business morality and refuses wealth obtained through unrighteous means."

Dr. Tong advocates the Confucian Ten Virtues (十德): rén 仁,yì 义,lǐ 礼,zhì 智,xìn 信,xiào 孝,tì 悌,zhōng 忠,lián 廉,chǐ 耻. This ten-virtue framework forms the direct foundation for the modern Eight Virtues of the RuShang (儒商八德). Dr. Tong stresses that wealth is not an end in itself. He holds the principle of shēng cái yǒu dào 生财有道 (create wealth in the proper way), and adopts Confucius's maxim "Wealth and honour obtained unrighteously are to me like floating clouds" as his personal motto.

For Dr. Tong, the RuShang pursues profit, but profit must serve benevolent purposes. This idea of yǐ cái fā shēn 以财发身 - using wealth to cultivate moral character aligns perfectly with the RuDao thesis: Confucian virtue forms the unchanging substance (体,tǐ) of enterprise, while adaptive business strategy constitutes the function (用,yòng). Dr. Tong describes this model as "The Analects plus the abacus", combining Confucian moral cultivation with practical commercial operation.

Dr. Tong said: "To be a RuShang is to uphold commercial morality and reject ill-gotten gains. Wealth acquired rightly is a means to carry out benevolent works. Wealth exists to cultivate one's moral character: we use wealth to develop benevolence."

"儒商就是要重视商业道德,不取不义之财,生财要取之有道,仁者生财是用来发展仁的事业,仁是儒家思想的核心,财是实现仁的事业的手段,故曰 以财发身"。

Source: Dr. Tong's Lecture at Sichuan University 四川大学 2005

Keywords of Dr. Tong: yǐ cái fā shēn 以财发身,righteous wealth, benevolence as purpose, wealth as instrument.

The Core Thought System of HuaXue-EnXue

HuaXue-EnXue carries on the orthodox current of Chinese culture, summarizing and developing Confucianism, Daoism, and Buddhism. Its core mission:

"To build, for the Chinese nation and indeed for all humanity, a system of thought and value by which to stand and live between heaven and earth. HuaXue-EnXue is the distillation of the finest elements of the world's cultures and religions - the village Way, village learning, and village rules of the global village; the family Way, family learning, and family rules of humanity's common earthly home."

The Core Ideas Explained

(1) Awaken to the Dao; root yourself; connect to the root.

"Devoutly awaken to the Dao; go deep in one discipline and take root - root in loyalty and filial piety fulfilled together; find the root of Chinese culture, the root of one's life and career. Solve the relation of Dao and technique from the root upward. Much past training only studied technique; now strive for the Dao. Only by learning, awakening, clarifying, and walking the Dao can technique finally be handled rightly, and can life and work return to their proper orbit."

RuShang reading: the inner-sage foundation - awaken to the Dao first, study technique second; cultivate the self first, manage the enterprise second.

(2) Before doing, learn to be human.

Professor Xiao stresses: "Before doing, learn to be human. Face life with a healthy mindset; find the root of Chinese culture and of one's life and career... Learn and carry forward the finest of Chinese culture; revive national education and national culture."

RuShang reading: the RuShang's core logic - self-cultivation as the root, enterprise management as the branch.

(3) Only reverence; no arrogance; no ego-attachment.

"Only reverence - no arrogance, no ego-attachment. Serve others; do not cling; do not overstep your station; do not promise lightly; do not take advantage; do not covet others' money or goods; feel neither aggrieved nor wounded."

RuShang reading: the RuShang's stance toward partners - humble, sincere, never overstepping, never grasping.

(4) Filial piety first among a hundred virtues.

"Filial piety is foremost of the hundred virtues. Be grateful to parents; seek their grace at the wellspring of life; teaching filial piety is the golden key that solves all problems. One must first achieve filial piety... Be grateful to every person who appears in your life; be good to people; only gratitude, no complaining; only reverence."

RuShang reading: the starting point of nation-and-society feeling - filial piety is the wellspring of gratitude, and gratitude the starting point of trust.

(5) Return to the scholar-merchant's devotion to family and nation.

"HuaXue-EnXue is devoted to carrying forward the finest of Chinese culture, bridging Chinese and Western management wisdom, transmitting the mission of enduring foundations, and returning to the scholar-merchant's devotion to family and nation - reconnecting to the living source of five thousand years of Huaxia culture... cultivating people of resolve who can love and serve the country and give their strength to the nation's rejuvenation and the sustained, healthy development of its economy."

RuShang reading: the ultimate mission - scholar and merchant as one; accountability to family and nation.

Principal Zhang Haibiao's Scholar-Merchant Theory 士商理论

At the ninth anniversary celebration of International HuaXue-EnXue (2020), themed "Cultivate deeply for nine years with goodwill; sprint for ten years to gain enlightenment," HuaXue family members from Italy, Spain, the United States, France, Australia, China, and beyond joined - nearly a thousand participants. Zhang Haibiao (Bill Zhang), head of the International HuaXue-EnXue Business School, has dedicated himself to bringing HuaXue-EnXue into business education.

The Business School's Scholar-Merchant Theory (ShiShangLiLun 士商理论) is precisely the modern expression of "RuShang and RuJiang": scholar-spirit, merchant-competence (ShiHunShangCai 士魂商才); Inner Sage, Outer King. It fuses the moral cultivation of the Confucian scholar with the commercial competence of the modern merchant - in full accord with this book's framework of "RuShang as Inner Sage, RuJiang as Outer King."

Reflection on HuaXue Studies: Poetic Grace, Refined Arts, and Cultivating Character Through Ritual and Music

(华学上课心得:诗情画意 琴棋书画 涵养心性 礼乐文脉润人生)

Author: Tony Tong (唐仪, RuDao.org / TonyTong.cc)
Program: HuaXue-EnXue Cohort 357 (华学357期)
Date: May 1-6, 2026 | Shenzhen (深圳)

1. Awakening to Ritual and Music (礼乐教化)

Attending Master Xiao Xianhua's (肖先华老师) lecture in Shenzhen was a transformative experience. He emphasized that true character requires both poetic sensibility and adherence to ritual and music (Li-Yue, 礼乐). Master Xiao highlighted how historical sages used the guqin (古琴) to cultivate inner peace, reshaping my understanding of China's four traditional arts: guqin, WeiQi, calligraphy, and painting (琴棋书画). As an amateur player of the piano, violin, and flute, I gained a deeper appreciation for how music and ritual interlock to form the bedrock of Chinese civilization.

2. The Core of Chinese Civilization (中华文明之本)

For five millennia, ritual (Li, 礼) has defined social order and conduct, while music (Yue, 乐) has nourished the soul. The guqin, as the pinnacle of ancient instruments, was an essential tool for self-cultivation among scholars and leaders. Master Xiao reminded us that historical accounts of sages mastering the zither are documented facts, reflecting the enduring tradition of using music to embody the Dao (Yi Qin Xiu Xin, Yi Yue Zai Dao, 以琴修心,以乐载道).

3. Three Historical Exemplars (三位圣贤典范)

  • Confucius (孔子): The founder of ritual-music culture studied under Music Master Xiang (师襄子). He refused to stop practicing until he grasped the moral character of King Wen (文王) embedded in the music. Even while facing starvation in Chen and Cai (陈蔡绝粮), he continued singing and playing the zither, demonstrating that music was a primary vehicle for cultivating virtue (Ren, 仁).
  • Zhuge Liang (诸葛亮 / 孔明): The prime minister of Shu Han (蜀汉名相) played the guqin to express his ambitions while farming in Longzhong (隆中). Records show he authored the Qin Jing (琴经, Zither Classic), detailing its theory and mechanics. His composure during crises stemmed directly from years of inner refinement through music.
  • Zhou Yu (周瑜 / 周郎): The legendary commander of Eastern Wu (东吴儒将) possessed absolute mastery of music (Qu You Wu, Zhou Lang Gu, 曲有误,周郎顾). Historical texts record that even after drinking, if a musician struck a wrong note, Zhou Yu would instantly notice and look up. He balanced military decisiveness with cultural refinement, embodying the ideal Confucian general (RuJiang, 儒将).

4. The Four Arts as Tools for Character Refinement (四艺修心)

Each of the four arts serves a specific purpose in character development:

  • Guqin (琴): Purifies the mind and calms impulse.
  • WeiQi/Go (棋): Expands strategic vision, foresight, and big-picture thinking.
  • Calligraphy (书): Disciplines posture and personal integrity.
  • Painting (画): Connects the spirit with nature and poetic vision.

Mastering instruments goes beyond technical skill; it is a discipline for preserving inner stability amid today's fast-paced world.

5. Commitment to Practice (知行合一)

Moving forward, I will follow Master Xiao's guidance and the example of ancient sages. I commit to practicing these traditional arts to refine my character, advance my musical skills, and embody the principles of ritual and music in daily governance and life.

Acknowledgments (致谢)

  • Master Xiao Xianhua (肖先华老师): My gratitude to Master Xiao for his teachings on ritual, music, and the zither, which inspired core concepts within the RuDao (儒道) framework.
  • Principal Zhang Haibiao (张海标校长): My sincere thanks to Principal Zhang for his profound insights into Confucian philosophy, the Confucian Entrepreneur (RuShang, 儒商), and the integration of ethics and profit (Yi-Li He-Yi, 义利合一) through co-opetition (竞合模式).

华学上课心得:诗情画意 琴棋书画 涵养心性 礼乐文脉润人生

作者:唐仪 TonyTong.cc - RuDao.org
HuaXue-EnXue 华学357期学员
2026年5月1-6日 于深圳

今日有幸在华学课堂聆听肖先华老师传道授业,心中豁然开朗,收获满满。老师谆谆教诲,为人处世当心怀诗情画意,更要恪守礼乐之道,懂乐明礼,修身养性,方能成就君子品格。课堂上,老师细数中华历代圣人先贤,谈及孔子,诸葛亮,周瑜皆为抚琴高手,以琴修心,以乐立身的史实,更让我对琴棋书画,礼乐文脉有了前所未有的深刻感悟。作为一名学习钢琴,小提琴,笛子多种乐器的爱好者,我更能体会到乐礼相融的文化底蕴,读懂中华传统文化的深厚魅力。

中华五千年文明绵延不绝,礼与乐始终是文化核心,立身之本。礼定秩序,规言行,让我们为人处世有尺有度;乐润心灵,养性情,让我们内心平和澄澈。而琴,自古便是乐之精髓,是文人圣贤,仁人君子不可或缺的修行载体,琴棋书画合称"文人四艺",更是刻在中国人血脉里的修身法门。肖老师课堂上提及的古圣先贤精通琴艺,并非文学演绎,而是有正史典籍明确记载的史实,每一段故事都印证着"以琴修心,以乐载道"的文化传统。

至圣先师孔子,是中华礼乐文化的奠基者,更是将琴艺与修身融为一体的圣人。据"史记-孔子世家""孔子家语"等正史记载,孔子曾拜鲁国乐官师襄子学琴,研习"文王操"时,他不满足于熟练弹奏曲调,掌握技法,而是潜心体悟琴中志向,直至在琴音中感知文王的圣贤人格,领悟仁德精髓才肯作罢。周游列国期间,孔子身陷陈蔡绝粮的困境,却依旧讲诵弦歌不衰,以琴乐安定弟子心神,坚守心中道义与理想。他提出"兴于诗,立于礼,成于乐",将琴乐纳入"六艺"教育体系,在孔子心中,弹琴从来不是单纯的技艺消遣,而是教化世人,涵养仁德,修身立德的根本途径。

三国名相诸葛亮,字孔明,亦是精通琴道,以琴明志的先贤典范。正史"三国志-诸葛亮传"明确记载,诸葛亮躬耕隆中时,好为"梁父吟",常抚琴吟唱,琴声中寄托着心系天下,济世安民的远大抱负;"襄阳耆旧记"亦有言,孔明常登高鼓瑟抚琴,寄情于乐;"中兴书目"更记载其著"琴经"一卷,详述制琴渊源与七弦乐理,是名副其实的音律大家。而"三国演义"中空城计焚香操琴的桥段,虽为文学演绎,却将诸葛亮以琴静心,临危不乱的气度刻画得淋漓尽致,这份从容智慧,正是常年抚琴修身,沉淀心性的成果。

东吴儒将周瑜,其琴艺造诣更是有正史铁证。"三国志-吴书-周瑜传"原文记载:"瑜少精意于音乐,虽三爵之后,其有阙误,瑜必知之,知之必顾。故时人谣曰:'曲有误,周郎顾。'"即便饮酒微醉,旁人弹琴稍有音误,他都能立刻察觉,足见其对音律,琴艺的精通程度。身为沙场将帅,周瑜以琴艺涵养儒雅风骨,兼具武将的果敢与文人的雅致,完美诠释了古人文武兼备,礼乐修身的君子风范。

三位先贤抚琴修身的史实,让我深刻明白:琴棋书画,艺艺皆修心。琴为心声,可涤荡浮躁,沉静内心;棋为智慧,可锤炼格局,思虑周全;书为心画,可端正品行,沉稳心性;画为意境,可感知天地,涵养诗情。而礼乐相伴,更是让这份修行有了根基,懂礼则言行方正,知乐则心境平和,这正是中华传统文化教给我们的做人之道。

平日里,我坚持学习钢琴,小提琴,笛子,在器乐演奏中感受音律之美,却未曾深入探寻背后的礼乐文脉与修身真谛。此次华学课堂,经肖老师点拨,才真正懂得,乐器演奏不只是技法的锤炼,更是传承礼乐文化,涵养自身心性的过程。当下社会节奏飞快,人心易浮躁,而琴棋书画与礼乐文化,正是让我们沉淀内心,回归本真的良方。

今后,我将牢记肖先华老师的教诲,以古圣先贤为榜样,深耕中华优秀传统文化,在琴棋书画中涵养心性,在礼乐文脉中砥砺品行。心怀诗情画意,恪守有礼有乐,既要精进乐器技艺,更要传承文化精髓,做礼乐文脉的践行者,华学精神的传承者,在传统文化的滋养中,修身立德,行稳致远。

感谢华学-恩学的肖先华老师,承蒙老师讲授华学,令我体悟礼乐与琴道的修身法门,启发本书儒道一体的核心思考。

同时感恩张海标校长,在儒家思想,儒商精神与中华传统文化层面,给予我关于"义利合一,竞合模式"的深刻启发。
