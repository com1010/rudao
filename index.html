For two and a half thousand years, strategists have studied The Art of War. This book asks a different question.

The Old Way
兵家之道

Win by Conquest

II.

About the Book

A business philosophy for leaders who refuse to choose between profit and principle.

In RuDao, Tony Tong weaves two and a half millennia of Confucian thought with the hard realities of modern commerce. The result is neither a self-help book nor a strategy manual — it is a way: a frame for decisions, partnerships, and growth that produces balanced profit and relationships that don't break when the cycle turns.

Drawing on his decades across finance, technology and cross-border enterprise, Tony shows how the Confucian virtues are not soft ideals but operational disciplines — the operating system of enduring business. The book is structured in three parts — Inner Sage (内圣篇), Outer King (外王篇), and Unity (融合篇) — spanning 18 chapters from the foundational meaning of 儒道 to the practical art of the co-opetition alliance.

长久以来，《孙子兵法》被奉为商业博弈的经典，教会人们战胜对手、夺取胜利。但以击败竞争者为目标的零和思维，常常摧毁合作根基、损耗信任、难以维系长期伙伴关系。《儒道-儒将》跳出单纯对抗的商战逻辑，阐释源自儒家思想的竞合之道：不必在利润和情义之间二选一，在激烈竞争的同时，依然可以构建稳固、长久的伙伴网络。

For generations, The Art of War has been revered as the bible of business competition. Yet zero-sum thinking focused on beating opponents often undermines cooperation, erodes trust, and makes long-lasting partnerships nearly impossible. RuDao moves beyond pure adversarial strategy and unfolds the Confucian path of co-opetition — profit and meaningful relationships do not need to be trade-offs.

Preface

RuDao: Beyond The Art of War

The Confucian Co-opetition Way to Balanced Profit and Enduring Partnerships

儒

When artificial intelligence, blockchain, and algorithms dominate global commerce, why should modern-day executives still turn to thinkers who lived two-and-a-half millennia ago: Confucius, Mencius, Lao-Tzu of the Tao-Te-Ching, and Sun Tzu of The Art of War? This question guided every stage of writing this book.

I was born in the beautiful mountains of Guilin, China, came to Hong Kong at an age of 10, came to Minnesota, USA, at an age of 18. I graduated in 1986 from King's College, Hong Kong — the alma-mater of Professor D.C. Lau (刘殿爵), the definitive English-language translator of The Analects, Tao-Te-Ching, and Mencius. Between 2024 and 2026 I deepened my classical studies through the HuaXue-EnXue (华学恩学) programme under Professor Xiao Xianhua (肖先华) and Principal Bill Zhang Haibiao (张海标). I also received insights from Taoist Master Ji Gong (季工) of the Hong Kong Taoism Institute, as well as inspiration from Dr. Tong Yan Kai (Tang EnJia 汤恩佳), President of the Hong Kong Confucian Academy.

My professional journey stretches from Web 1.0 through to today's emerging Web 4.0. In 2000 I co-founded Pacificnet, a Nasdaq-listed enterprise, and I hold a United States patent for internet workflow technology. I now lecture on AI, blockchain and Web3 at multiple universities and have presented at industry summits hosted by Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan, UBS and other leading global financial institutions.

This dual-lived experience — steeped both in traditional Chinese humanistic thought and frontier-era global finance-technology in the USA — has convinced me: Confucian-Taoist wisdom is no static museum exhibit. It delivers urgently-needed governance and leadership frameworks for our digital-intelligence age.

The Art of War by Sun Tzu has for generations been embraced as a business-strategy bible, centered on outwitting and defeating rivals. Yet zero-sum adversarial mindsets eat away at trust, undermine cooperative foundations, and render lasting partnerships fragile and unsustainable.

RuDao sets forth an alternative vision: leaders do not need to make an either-or choice between profit and integrity. It is entirely possible to compete fiercely in the marketplace while building robust, long-lived partner ecosystems.

Inner virtue in isolation cannot sustain an organization amid unforgiving global competition. Clever stratagem untethered from ethical foundations will collapse over time. Ru-Shang (儒商 — Confucian Entrepreneurs) and Ru-Jiang (儒将 — Confucian Strategist-Leader) must function as one unified system, further balanced by Dao / Tao (道 — Tao-Te-Ching) insights for fluid, adaptive strategic action.

III.

The Structure of the Way

三篇十八章 — Three parts, eighteen chapters, from the inner sage to the enduring alliance.

Part I

内圣篇 · The Inner Sage — 儒商

The RuShang's foundation of virtue — from 义以生利 to the eight virtues. Why trust, not tactics, is the merchant's first asset.

Ch. 1

儒商本源 — The Origin of RuShang: Confucius, ZiGong and 义以生利

Where the Confucian merchant begins: Confucius's own disciple 子贡, and the classical formula 义以生利 — righteousness generates profit, rather than opposing it.

Ch. 2

儒商八德 — The Eight Virtues: 仁 义 礼 智 信 忠 孝 恕

A quotation system for the eight Confucian virtues — 仁 benevolence, 义 righteousness, 礼 propriety, 智 wisdom, 信 trust, 忠 loyalty, 孝 filial piety, 恕 reciprocity — and how each one actually operates in business.

Ch. 3

儒学演进 — The Evolution of Confucianism: From 周虽旧邦，其命维新 to Modern New Confucianism

How a 2,500-year tradition renews itself — from Confucius and Mencius through Dong Zhongshu to the modern New Confucians, and why a living tradition can still guide a digital economy.

Ch. 4

儒商标杆 — Nine Exemplars Across 2,500 Years: From 子贡 to 稻盛和夫

Six Chinese and three Japanese exemplars — from 子贡 to 稻盛和夫, 涩泽荣一 and 松下幸之助 — read side by side to find the repeatable pattern behind enduring business virtue.

Ch. 5

儒家诚信 — Confucian Trustworthiness: From 民无信不立 to Cross-Border Credit Systems

Why 信 is the one asset that compounds without depreciating — and how classical trustworthiness maps onto modern credit, compliance, and cross-border reputation.

Ch. 6

核心过渡 — The Pivotal Question: Why Virtue Alone Cannot Survive the Market

Inner virtue is necessary but not sufficient. The turning point of the book: why the RuShang must also become the RuJiang — the Confucian merchant must learn to lead in competition.

Part II

外王篇 · The Outer King — 儒将

From inner virtue to outward strategy — the RuJiang's co-opetition path beyond Sun Tzu: negotiation, alliance, crisis, and the ancient scholar-generals.

Ch. 7

竞合之道 — The Boundary of Sun Tzu and the Way of Co-opetition

Where The Art of War stops being useful: 不战而屈人之兵 read as a discipline, not a boast. How zero-sum strategy erodes the trust that durable partnerships require.

Ch. 8

儒道五德 — The Five Constants in Market Competition: 仁 义 礼 智 信

The five virtues turned into operating disciplines — how 仁义礼智信 translate into pricing, negotiation, dispute resolution, and long-horizon investment decisions.

Ch. 9

儒将谈判与同盟 — RuJiang Negotiation and Alliance

Negotiation as relationship-building rather than extraction — the move from transaction to alliance, worked through cross-border and Southeast Asian case scenarios.

Ch. 10

儒将危机 — The RuJiang in Crisis: 岁寒知松柏

Character shows under pressure. How to hold both 道义 and partnership through the worst quarter — the 岁寒知松柏 test, and what it costs to pass it.

Ch. 11

中国古代儒将 — Ancient RuJiang Wisdom: 诸葛亮、刘备、曹操、周瑜

Four scholar-generals read as strategists rather than heroes: 诸葛亮's restraint, 刘备's alliance-building, 曹操's pragmatism, and 周瑜's fatal imbalance.

Ch. 12

儒将实战案例集 — The RuJiang Casebook

Field cases in four modes — genuine co-opetition, crisis leadership, the traps of 伪竞合 pseudo-co-opetition, and the cross-cultural misreadings that break alliances.

Part III

融合篇 · The Unity — 内圣外王

Where inner sage and outer king converge — a complete commercial system for the digital age: AI governance, Web3, and a new Confucian business civilization.

Ch. 13

内圣外王 — Inner Sage, Outer King: The Philosophical Unity

The synthesis the whole book has been building toward: 内圣 and 外王 are not two roles but one discipline — the RuShang and the RuJiang are one person.

Ch. 14

儒家德性伦理与AI治理 — Confucian Virtue Ethics and AI Business Governance

Virtue ethics as an AI governance framework — 仁 as design constraint, 义 as alignment test, 信 as accountability — for enterprises deploying automated decisions.

Ch. 15

儒家诚信与Web3 — Confucian Trustworthiness and Web3, DAO and the Token Economy

What blockchain changes and what it cannot: 信 between strangers without an intermediary — and why code-enforced trust still needs a moral foundation.

Ch. 16

文明对话 — Dialogue of Civilizations and the New Confucian Business Civilization

Toynbee's warning and Ikeda's answer, read toward a Web4 horizon: what a Confucian-rooted business civilization could contribute to global governance.

Ch. 17

华学恩学 — HuaXue-EnXue: A Complete Modern RuShang Practice System

A concrete contemporary practice system — 张海标校长's 士商理论 — turning the book's principles into an operable personal curriculum.

Ch. 18

三十年见证 — A Thirty-Year Practitioner's Witness: From Web1.0 to Web4

The author's own thirty years across four technology waves — closing the loop between classical principle and lived practice, and where the reader's own thirty years begin.

V.

The Five Pillars

五常 · The Five Constants at the heart of RuDao

仁

Ren · Benevolence

Treat partners, employees and competitors as people, not positions. Profit earned with kindness compounds.

义

Yi · Righteousness

The courage to walk away from the deal that should never be made. Reputation is built in the deals you refuse.

礼

Li · Propriety

Form, ritual, the right words at the right time. In business, this is the architecture of trust.

智

Zhi · Wisdom

Knowing what to do, and — harder — what not to do. The strategic restraint of the sage.

信

Xin · Trust

The currency that outlasts every other. Enduring partnerships are built on the trust you cannot buy back.

VI.

Three Principles of Co-opetition

三大原则 — The operational core of RuDao, distilled from sixteen chapters into three disciplines.

01
义利共存

Profit & Trust Coexist

Profit is not the enemy of principle — greed is. When 义 (righteousness) and 利 (profit) are aligned, each reinforces the other. The Confucian merchant does not sacrifice margin for morals; he designs deals where the moral path is also the profitable one.

利润与信任共存：义利合一是儒商的根基。

02
竞合并行

Competition & Cooperation in Parallel

Compete where the market is real; cooperate where the value is shared. The modern enterprise is not a zero-sum arena but a layered ecosystem. RuDao teaches the strategic intelligence to know which layer you are in — and which mode to deploy.

竞争与合作并行：在竞争中寻求合作，在合作中保持竞争力。

03
共赢联盟

Win-Win Alliances

The goal is not to defeat the rival but to grow the pie together. Enduring partnerships — between companies, across borders, between founders and investors — are built when both sides define success as mutual flourishing, not unilateral victory.

共赢联盟：不是击败对手，而是共同做大蛋糕。

RuDao does not discard Sun Tzu. It absorbs him. The Art of War remains the sharpest tool for the zero-sum layer — the battle you must win. But RuDao asks: must every encounter be a battle? The answer, confirmed by three decades of cross-border commerce, is no. The greater game is the one where the rival becomes a partner and the partnership outlasts the deal.

VIII.

The Lineage

儒商 · 儒将 · 天人合一 — Three traditions, one way

RuDao does not invent a philosophy. It braids three strands of Chinese thought into a single cord strong enough for modern commerce — the virtue of the scholar-merchant, the strategy of the scholar-general, and the Taoist insight that heaven and humanity are one.

儒商
The Business Ideal

Confucian Merchant

Commerce conducted with integrity, not despite it. The 儒商 trades in trust as much as in goods — believing that a deal well made serves both counterparties, and that reputation is the only balance sheet that never needs auditing.

儒将
The Strategic Ideal

Confucian General

Power tempered by learning, force guided by virtue. The 儒将 wins without burning the field he intends to harvest — competing fiercely where stakes are real, but never mistaking a rival for an enemy.

天人合一
The Philosophical Root

Unity of Heaven & Humanity

From Taoism: the conviction that 天人合一 — heaven, earth and people form a single living system. Business is not separate from life; profit is not separate from people. Enduring success flows only from alignment with this whole.

Tony integrates Confucian ethics with modern global business strategy. He writes as a 儒商 — a Confucian merchant who believes commerce and conscience are not opposites — and as a student of the 儒将 tradition, the scholar-general who wields strategy without surrendering virtue. RuDao draws these lineages together with the Taoist insight of 天人合一 — the unity of heaven and humanity — to offer a way of business that is balanced, enduring, and whole.
